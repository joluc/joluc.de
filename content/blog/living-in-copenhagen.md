# Copenhagen Notes — Observations from a Few Weeks

I got the opportunity to work remotely from Copenhagen for a few weeks. What started as a change of scenery turned into a masterclass in intentional urban design. These are my observations, written down as I experienced them — the small details that made me stop and think about how cities could be.

## The Bicycle City

**Day 3:** I rented a bike today. Copenhagen isn't just bike-friendly — it's bike-first, and the difference is immediately obvious.

### Infrastructure That Thinks Ahead

The cycling infrastructure here shows a level of foresight I haven't seen elsewhere. Construction sites have dedicated bike detours, clearly marked. I noticed bike lanes get snow removal priority — even before car lanes. At traffic lights, there are small footrests so you can balance without putting your foot down. Trash cans are angled perfectly for throwing away coffee cups while riding.

**Day 5:** I learned a small gesture today: when you stop at a red light, you raise your hand slightly to signal to cyclists behind you. Everyone does it. It's a tiny act of communication that prevents chain reactions and shows mutual respect.

And something I never thought about: **no tram tracks**. No dangerous grooves that catch your bike wheels. It makes cycling feel genuinely safe.

## The Metro Culture

**Day 2:** Something remarkable happened at the metro station. Everyone — and I mean everyone — stands to the side of doors to let people exit first. Not most people. Everyone. This isn't enforced; it's just how things are done.

The metro runs 24/7 with autonomous trains that are remarkably reliable. Combined with bikes and harbor ferries, mobility here has a predictability that reduces daily stress in ways I didn't expect.

### Art Meets Infrastructure

**Day 8:** At Havnholmen metro station, I found something delightful: functional clocks, display boards, and check-in gates installed upside-down on the ceiling as public art. The fact that this was legally possible shows both humor and courage. It's playful, intentional, and somehow perfectly Copenhagen.

## Water Everywhere

**Day 4:** You can swim in the city canals. I watched people sunbathe along the harbor during lunch breaks and jump into the water. In winter (I'm told), popup saunas appear in every district, and people run from the heat into the freezing canal water.

The city has a high density of saunas with ocean access — a luxury that feels both Nordic and deeply civilized.

## Aesthetic Intentionality

**Week 2:** There's a consistent aesthetic thread running through Copenhagen's public and private spaces. Decisions feel **intentional** — from architecture to street furniture to how shops are designed.

### Clean Surfaces

I've noticed graffiti, tags, and stickers are consistently removed. Not aggressively, but persistently. The result is a city that feels cared for without feeling sterile. Public spaces remain legible and beautiful.

### The Written Language

Danish has this contemporary art feeling to it — the written language looks elegant and minimalist on signs, menus, and posters. The pronunciation, though? Wonderfully weird and expressive. There's an aesthetic pleasure in just seeing the text.

## Hygge in Practice

**Evening walks:** Hygge isn't just a marketing term here. Walking through residential neighborhoods in the evening, I see candles and flowers in almost every window.

**Friday observation:** I keep passing cyclists carrying fresh bouquets home for the weekend — **fredagsblomster** (Friday flowers). It's a small ritual that marks the transition to rest.

Inside cafés and the apartment I'm staying in, lighting is treated as an art form. Multiple light sources at different heights, warm tones, intentional shadows. It's not just cozy — it's a sophisticated approach to creating atmosphere.

## Food Culture

### Supermarkets Done Right

The supermarket layouts prioritize fresh, healthy food. There's no over-representation of candy in aisles designed to trap you. Fresh vegetables are relatively affordable. Even the choice to keep cucumbers wrapped in plastic is intentional — it dramatically reduces food waste.

At waste collection points, there are free trash bags for sorting. Recycling isn't just encouraged; it's made easy.

### Bolle med Ost (BMO)

**Morning ritual:** I've adopted the BMO culture: sourdough rolls with butter and cheese. Simple, high-quality, ubiquitous. It's the kind of food culture that values craft and tradition without pretension.

### Sunday Shopping & Great Restaurants

Supermarkets, bakeries, and larger stores are open on Sundays. Coming from Germany, this feels liberating.

The density of excellent restaurants, cafés, and bars is remarkable for a city this size. The food scene punches well above its weight.

## Water Quality

**Week 1:** The tap water here is exceptional — clean, fresh-tasting, and safe. At the coworking space, there are carafes of tap water on every table. No one buys bottled water. People take pride in this.

## Social Fabric

### Everyday Kindness

**Daily interactions:** There's a baseline friendliness in daily interactions — at bakeries, supermarkets, on the metro, in traffic. It's not effusive or performative; it's just a pleasant, respectful way of moving through the world together.

### Safety and Trust

**Observation:** I feel safe here. Bikes are secured with simple wheel locks, not heavy chains. I don't see visible homelessness, even around the central station. There's a general lack of mistrust about theft.

This isn't about ignoring social issues — it's about a society that has largely succeeded in providing basic security and dignity.

### The Flag

**Curious detail:** Danes have a positive relationship with their flag. I've seen small Danish flags on birthday tables, in gardens, at celebrations. It's not nationalistic — it's a symbol of togetherness and celebration. It feels healthy.

## Traffic Lights That Respect Your Time

**Cycling observation:** Traffic light timing is optimized for flow. Cycles are short, and I rarely wait long. For cyclists especially, the green waves are timed so you can ride through multiple intersections without stopping. It's another small detail that shows respect for people's time.

## Proximity to Nature

**Weekend trip:** Being close to Sweden opens up incredible nature access. Malmö is a bridge away. I took a day trip to Lund, and Swedish national parks are easily reachable for berry picking, mushroom foraging, hiking, and cycling tours. It has a Finnish wilderness feeling, but it's right next door.

## What I'm Taking Away

**Final thoughts:** These few weeks in Copenhagen have shown me that urban life doesn't have to be a compromise. When a city is designed with intention — when infrastructure, culture, and social norms align around making daily life better — the result is transformative.

It's not perfect. It's expensive. The weather has been gray. But the quality of daily life, the thoughtfulness embedded in systems, and the sense that people genuinely care about their shared spaces creates something rare: a city that feels like it's designed for humans, not despite them.

I'm writing this from a café in Nørrebro, watching cyclists pass by with their Friday flowers. In a few days, I'll be back home, but these observations will stay with me.

**Copenhagen isn't just a city I visited. It's a proof of concept for what cities could be.**
